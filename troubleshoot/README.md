## ✎ Make your success known. Keeping your head down and staying quiet is not a strategy. I had to learn to find my voice in meetings where I was scared to speak up, make my wins visible to leadership, and build relationships with the people who needed to know my name. I didn't walk into the company knowing how to do that, but it's where I learned the power of self-advocacy.

## ✎ Just because you're good at something doesn't mean it's good for you. I excelled in roles that burned me out. The moment I recognized that my performance and my wellbeing were moving in opposite directions, I made changes. Walking away from a role or situation to put yourself first is the best thing you can do for yourself.

## ✎ Trust in yourself and your inner knowing. When well-intentioned leaders cautioned me about accepting my employee-focused role, I made the choice that felt right and it changed my career trajectory. You know yourself best and you have to choose the work that feels most aligned with what you want.

## ✎ Let your values guide your choices. Nothing brought that into sharper focus than becoming a father. Suddenly the work I was doing, the hours I was working, and the energy I was spending all went through a different filter. When your choices stop reflecting what you actually value, you'll feel it. And the longer you ignore it, the louder it gets.

## ✎ The network you cultivate will have your back when it matters most. When you show up for your people consistently, they'll do the same for you, advocating for you and believing you before you fully believe in yourself. Salesforce provided me with so much, but it's the people I'll miss the most. To those who shaped my story, thank you doesn't begin to cover it.
